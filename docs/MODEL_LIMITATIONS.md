# MODEL LIMITATIONS

**CORPUS IS NOT FOR CLINICAL USE. IT IS AN EDUCATIONAL SIMULATION.**

The fidelity target is *"a medical student says that's roughly right"*, not *"a
cardiologist says that's clinically valid"*. This document is the complete list of
places where the model is an approximation, a simplification, or diverges from real
physiology. It is maintained from Phase 1 onward, per spec §0.7, and it is linked
from the application's footer.

Anything the engine could not source is in [`MISSING_CONSTANTS.md`](./MISSING_CONSTANTS.md),
which is generated by the ingestion pipeline. This document covers the *modelling*
choices; that one covers the *data* gaps.

---

## 1. The whole body

| Limitation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **One body, partly resizable.** A 70 kg, 1.73 m reference adult. Mass now scales drug disposition allometrically (volumes x mass, clearances x mass^0.75, `core/body.ts`), and age sets the exercise heart-rate ceiling. | The CIRCULATION stays reference-sized: blood volume, compliances and chamber elastances are sourced for the 70 kg adult, and pouring a different volume into the same vessels is a haemorrhage or an overload rather than a different-sized person (the first attempt did exactly that). Sex, ethnicity, comorbidity and genetics change nothing. |
| **No disease states.** There is no heart failure, no COPD, no cirrhosis, no renal impairment as a *condition* — only as a consequence of something you did. | You cannot set up a patient; you can only perturb a healthy one. |
| **Little autonomic adaptation.** The baroreflex resets its operating pressure during exercise (central command), but has no long-term resetting or gain adaptation. | A sustained pressor produces a sustained reflex response; in reality the reflex resets over hours. |
| **Pain, fright, stress, low mood, sleep and exercise are single scalars.** | Each drives the sympathetic and HPA axes through the effect bus, but there is no personality, habituation or learning: the tenth fright is as large as the first. |
| **Compartment mixing is instantaneous.** A drug injected into the central compartment is uniformly distributed within it in one tick. | Very early distribution kinetics (the first 10–20 s after a bolus) are faster than reality. |

## 2. Cardiovascular

- **Eight lumped compartments.** There is no arterial tree, no wave reflection, no
  regional circulation. Splanchnic, cerebral, coronary and renal flows are computed
  as fractions of cardiac output rather than as their own circuits.
- **No valve lesions.** Valves are ideal diodes with a forward resistance. There is
  no regurgitation, no stenosis and therefore no murmur.
- **No regional wall motion, no coronary anatomy.** Ischaemia is not modelled. You
  cannot produce a myocardial infarction.
- **Ventricular interdependence is absent.** The septum is not shared between the two
  elastance models, so the right ventricle does not impede the left when it dilates.
- **Right ventricular activation is simultaneous with the left.** In reality it leads
  slightly.
- **The explicit Frank–Starling term is small and deliberately so.** The elastance
  model already produces a Starling response geometrically; the extra factor models
  length-dependent calcium sensitivity, which is genuinely additional. A large gain
  would double-count.
- **The right atrium does not distend as much as a real one during arrest.** As a
  result the model's coronary perfusion pressure during optimal CPR sits at the top
  of the reported human range (roughly 25–50 mmHg where 10–30 is typical). The
  *direction* and the threshold behaviour are right — good compressions clear the
  15 mmHg that predicts return of spontaneous circulation and no compressions do
  not — but the absolute number is optimistic.
- **Systemic vascular resistance has one value.** There is no separate arteriolar,
  capillary and venular resistance, so a drug cannot redistribute flow between beds
  except through the explicit `renal.vascularResistance` target.
- **AV-nodal block is a proportional cut in ventricular rate.** There are no separate
  atrial and ventricular clocks, so a drug's AV-nodal effect lowers the rate even in
  sinus rhythm, where the real effect is a longer PR interval. Digoxin's rate effect in
  sinus rhythm is overstated for this reason.

## 3. Electrocardiogram

- **The McSharry–Clifford model is a *descriptive* generator, not an electrical
  model of the heart.** It produces a trace with the right morphology; it does not
  know about conduction pathways, so there is no bundle branch block, no
  pre-excitation and no axis deviation.
- **The S wave is deeper than lead II usually shows.** This is an artefact of the
  overlapping Gaussian events at the published Q/R/S parameters: the events are only
  0.26 rad apart with a width of 0.1, so their tails interact and z does not return
  to baseline between them. The R wave, the rate and the rhythm are correct.
- **Amplitude is scaled, not derived.** The model's `z` is dimensionless; the paper
  scales its output to a physiological range rather than emitting millivolts. CORPUS
  applies `ecg.amplitudeGain` to do the same, and additionally normalises by ω so
  the R wave does not shrink as the heart speeds up — which the raw formulation
  would otherwise do.
- **Hyperkalaemia is cosmetic.** Serum potassium above 5.5 mEq/L scales the trace
  amplitude; it does not genuinely peak the T wave or widen the QRS by altering the
  event parameters.
- **VF is band-limited noise, not a re-entry model.** Its dominant frequency and
  amplitude decay are taken from published VF spectra, but nothing about it is
  derived from myocardial electrophysiology.

## 4. Respiratory

- **A single alveolar compartment with a shunt fraction.** Shunt is now an explicit
  variable (the shunt equation; pneumonia and anaphylaxis write it), but there is still
  no ventilation–perfusion distribution, no dead-space disease and no diffusion
  limitation, so a V/Q-mismatch hypoxaemia that oxygen corrects fully cannot be told
  apart from a shunt that it does not.
- **No airway mechanics.** No resistance, no compliance, no work of breathing. Tidal
  volume is set by drive, not by muscle against a load.
- **Intubation is a switch.** It sets a fixed rate, tidal volume and FiO₂ 1.0. There
  is no ventilator mode, no PEEP and no auto-PEEP.
- **Carbon dioxide is one store.** A rise is limited by mass balance against a
  whole-body capacitance (3.4 mmHg/min in complete apnoea, Stock 1989); a fall by lung
  washout. The real store is multi-compartment, and the ~12 mmHg first-minute jump of
  an apnoea with a circulation (lung and blood equilibrating with mixed venous gas) is
  not reproduced.
- **Acid–base is Henderson–Hasselbalch on bicarbonate, not a strong-ion model.**
  pH follows the live PaCO₂, lactate and ketoacids (buffered 1:1) and a renal
  compensation on a one-day clock (`systems/acidbase.ts`). There is no chloride or
  albumin arithmetic of Stewart's kind, metabolic-alkalosis respiratory compensation is
  deliberately weak, and acetazolamide's bicarbonate diuresis does NOT produce its
  metabolic acidosis - so its altitude benefit is absent. (A `resp.drive` shortcut that
  stood in for it was removed on 2026-09-25: it acted within minutes where the real
  acidosis takes hours.)
- **Agonal respiration plateaus instead of ceasing.** After a cardiac arrest the model
  settles at roughly 1.7 breaths a minute at about 100 mL and stays there. Real agonal
  gasping fades over a few minutes and stops. The recognition cue is right - this is
  obviously not normal breathing - but the endpoint is not, and a long downtime will
  show gasps that should have gone (ADR-024).
- **There is no intracranial pressure.** Cerebral perfusion pressure is computed as mean
  arterial minus CENTRAL VENOUS pressure, which is the available proxy. Raised ICP,
  and therefore every way it changes cerebral perfusion, is not modelled.

- **A respiratory depressant reduces drive; it does not blunt the CO₂ response curve.**
  Opioids and benzodiazepines really act by flattening the slope of the ventilatory
  response to CO₂ and shifting its apnoeic threshold. This model scales total drive
  instead, which reproduces the important consequence — ventilation settles at a higher
  PaCO₂ — but not the change in the *shape* of the response. In particular, the
  chemoreflex here retains its full gain, so a depressed body defends its CO₂ more
  briskly than a real one would. See ADR-021; this is also why the effect must be
  applied to drive rather than to rate, and why applying it to rate made midazolam
  do nothing at all.
- **Steady-state ventilation always returns to metabolic demand.** That is a property
  of the alveolar ventilation equation and it is correct, but it means the respiratory
  *rate* recovers after a depressant while the PaCO₂ stays elevated. Read the rate
  alone and a drug can look as though it has worn off when it has not.

## 5. Renal

- **One nephron population, one filtration equation.** There is no tubular
  segmentation, no countercurrent multiplier, no individual electrolyte handling.
- **Urine composition is not modelled.** Only volume is.
- **No micturition reflex.** The bladder fills to capacity and stays there. Over a
  24-hour run it reaches 500 mL and stops.
- **Creatinine is a single-compartment tracer** with constant production. It
  therefore lags an acute GFR change by hours, which is correct, but it has no muscle
  mass dependence.
- **Diuretic action is a single tubular-permeability term.** There is no distinction
  between loop, thiazide and potassium-sparing sites, and no electrolyte
  consequences beyond volume.

---

## 6. Gastrointestinal

- **Twelve segments with fixed transit times**, except the stomach, which uses the
  Elashoff power-exponential. Real transit is driven by migrating motor complexes
  and varies enormously.
- **No enterohepatic circulation, no gut microbiome, no bile.**
- **Digestion is carbohydrate-only.** Fat and protein affect gastric emptying and
  acid buffering but are not absorbed or metabolised.
- **Glucose transport is a single whole-gut SGLT1 capacity.** It is correctly a
  property of the epithelium rather than of each bolus — an earlier version applied
  the maximum per parcel, which let ten parcels deliver ten times the physiological
  maximum — but there is no GLUT2 recruitment and no incretin effect.
- **Gastric pH is derived from free acid over volume**, with protein as the only
  buffer. There is no pepsin, no intrinsic factor and no gastrin feedback.
- **There is no gastric capacity limit, no satiety and no vomiting.** You can put
  2.5 L into a stomach whose comfortable capacity is about 1 L, and the model will
  simply hold it. The fill fraction the renderer uses is clamped at 1.0, so the
  organ looks full rather than looking wrong, but the volume chip will show a
  number a real stomach would have rejected.
- **Oral drug absorption is site-specific by a fixed profile**, not by pH
  partitioning, permeability or transporter expression.

---

- **Nausea and vomiting are not states this model has.** There is no emetic drive, so an
  antiemetic has nothing to act on. Ondansetron's 5-HT₃ blockade reaches the model only
  through `gi.motility`, where it is correctly constipating and clinically beside the
  point; metoclopramide's antiemetic half is absent entirely and only its prokinetic half
  is modelled (ADR-023). Giving either drug in CORPUS will not look like giving it to a
  patient, because the reason you would give it does not exist here.
- **Gastric emptying responds to volume and osmolarity far more than to motility.** A
  motility modifier therefore has modest leverage: metoclopramide empties the stomach
  about 1 % faster over twenty minutes rather than the substantially faster emptying the
  label describes. The direction is right and the magnitude is understated.

## 7. Pharmacokinetics

- **Two or three compartments, integrated as ODEs.** Correct for the drugs modelled;
  wrong for anything with genuinely non-linear distribution.
- **Hepatic clearance scales with cardiac output** for every drug, as though all were
  high-extraction. Low-extraction drugs are in reality insensitive to hepatic blood
  flow and sensitive to enzyme capacity instead.
- **No metabolites.** Morphine-6-glucuronide, for instance, is a more potent analgesic
  than morphine and is not modelled at all.
- **No enzyme induction or inhibition, no drug–drug pharmacokinetic interactions.**
- **Protein binding is a constant fraction.** It does not saturate, and drugs do not
  displace one another from albumin.
- **First-pass extraction is applied once**, either as a bioavailability factor or as
  an extraction ratio, never both.
- **Several central volumes are `derived`, not measured.** Where a label quotes only
  a volume of distribution, clearance and half-life, `V1` is inferred from the
  reported distribution phase. Each such case is listed in `MISSING_CONSTANTS.md`
  §4 with the reasoning shown.
- **Where a published Vd turned out to be V_area rather than Vss**, the
  two-compartment system is insoluble and the model falls back to one compartment at
  V_area. The early distribution phase after a bolus is then not reproduced for that
  drug. Those drugs are listed in `MISSING_CONSTANTS.md` §2.

## 7a. Routes of administration

Twelve routes are modelled. `src/data/routes.json` carries a citation, a confidence
and a note for each; this section records what the whole scheme cannot do.

- **A route is modelled as a barrier, not as a place.** Each extravascular route is a
  first-order depot whose rate constant is a fixed multiple of the drug's measured
  intramuscular one. There is no tissue compartment for the injection site, no
  dependence of absorption on local blood flow, and therefore no feedback from the
  drug onto its own absorption. That last omission has a named victim: subcutaneous
  adrenaline is slow partly *because* adrenaline constricts the vessels around its own
  depot, and the model reproduces that only as a fixed rate constant taken from
  Simons (2001), not as the mechanism.

- **Several route scale factors are engineering defaults.** Subcutaneous (0.8x) and
  sublingual (1.5x) are not measured ratios — they are the generic case, and their
  `confidence` says `assumed`. Where a real product publishes real figures, that
  product overrides the default and the override cites its label: adrenaline
  subcutaneous, naloxone nasal, fentanyl nasal, sublingual and transdermal.

- **THE ADRENALINE SUBCUTANEOUS TIME TO PEAK IS COMPRESSED, AND CANNOT BE FIXED BY
  CHANGING THE RATE CONSTANT.** Simons measured 8 ± 2 min intramuscularly and
  34 ± 14 min subcutaneously in the same adults. The model reproduces the four-fold
  difference in the *rate constant* exactly, but its simulated subcutaneous peak
  arrives at about 12 min rather than 34, and no absorption rate would move it much
  further: adrenaline is modelled in one compartment with a 2 min disposition
  half-life, and such a drug peaks no later than about 30 min however slowly it is
  fed in. Reaching 34 min honestly would need a distribution phase that adrenaline's
  cited pharmacokinetics do not identify. What the model does show correctly is the
  part that changed practice — subcutaneous is later and lower.

- **A depot has no volume and no upper limit.** Ten times the reference dose goes into
  the same depot as a tenth of it and absorbs at the same rate constant. Real
  intramuscular and subcutaneous absorption slow as the depot volume grows, and there
  is a volume above which the site simply will not take it.

- **Intraosseous is intravenous plus a 15-second transit.** The delay is an
  engineering value, not a measurement. It does produce one correct and non-obvious
  consequence — for adenosine, whose half-life is six seconds, that transit costs
  roughly half the peak concentration — but the number driving it is chosen, not
  cited.

- **The rectal first-pass split is anatomical, not measured.** Roughly half the rectal
  venous drainage is portal and half systemic, so the model applies half the hepatic
  extraction ratio. Where the dose actually lands in the rectum decides the real
  split, and nothing here models that; de Boer's review is emphatic that rectal
  absorption is the most variable of any route, and the model shows a single
  deterministic curve.

- **Transdermal has a fixed 12-hour lag and no removal.** The patch is applied and
  releases on a schedule; there is no way to take it off, and no residual skin depot
  is modelled separately from the releasing one. The lag and the persistence are the
  educational point of the route, and they are reproduced; the ability to intervene
  is not.

- **A nebuliser is a zero-order pulmonary input over a fixed ten minutes.** Nothing
  models breathing pattern, device efficiency, or the large fraction of a real
  nebulised dose that is swallowed rather than inhaled.

- **No route is modelled as failing.** Every injection is correctly placed, every
  tablet is swallowed, every patch adheres. Extravasation, a missed vein, a spat-out
  tablet and a patch that falls off are all ordinary clinical events and none of them
  can happen here.

## 7b. Dose scaling

- **The multiplier is bounded at 0.1x to 10x of a cited reference dose**, enforced in
  the worker rather than in the interface. Outside that range the model stops being
  informative rather than becoming wrong: the pharmacokinetics are linear, so a
  hundred-fold dose produces a hundred-fold concentration and teaches only that the
  model is linear.

- **Because the kinetics are linear, so is every dose response in the plasma.** Real
  saturable elimination, saturable protein binding and saturable absorption all flatten
  the top of the dose-exposure curve, and none of them are modelled except where a
  drug declares explicit Michaelis-Menten elimination. A 10x dose really does produce a
  10x plasma concentration here, and for most real drugs at most real doses it would
  not.

- **The pharmacodynamic response does saturate**, because receptor occupancy saturates
  and the effect curve is a Hill function of it. So the *concentration* scales
  linearly and the *effect* does not, which is the right shape — but the ceiling comes
  entirely from the receptor model, and nothing represents the toxic effects that a
  large overdose causes through mechanisms the model has no receptor for.

## 7c. The expanded drug set

The set grew from 19 drugs to 89. Most of what follows is a consequence of breadth
rather than of any one entry.

- **Most of the new drugs are ONE-compartment.** Where an FDA label gives a volume of
  distribution and a half-life and nothing else, that is exactly one compartment, and a
  two-compartment fit would need a central volume nobody measured. The early distribution
  phase after a bolus is therefore not reproduced for those drugs. Each says so in its
  own provenance note.

- **Nuclear receptors act far too quickly.** The glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid
  receptors work by changing transcription, over hours. They are applied here with the
  same instantaneous occupancy every other receptor uses. Direction and relative size are
  right; onset is not.

- **Irreversible binding is modelled as reversible.** Aspirin acetylates
  cyclo-oxygenase covalently and omeprazole binds the proton pump covalently; in both
  cases the effect outlasts the drug by days and the duration has nothing to do with the
  half-life. The model has only reversible kinetics, so it understates both
  substantially.

- **Enzyme induction and inhibition are absent**, which matters more now than it did.
  Carbamazepine induces its own metabolism; MDMA inhibits its own through CYP2D6, making
  its kinetics markedly non-linear above about 100 mg. The model is linear and therefore
  UNDERSTATES MDMA exposure at higher multipliers. That is the most clinically
  significant single approximation in the expanded set.

- **Ion-channel and receptor subtypes are collapsed into families.** `nav` covers
  Nav1.1 through Nav1.8, `cav` covers the L-type family, `herg` covers the delayed
  rectifiers. Real subtype selectivity — Nav1.5 cardiac versus Nav1.7 nociceptive — is
  therefore not representable, and a subtype-selective drug cannot be distinguished from
  a non-selective one by affinity alone. Where the distinction is clinically decisive,
  the drug carries it as a direct effect instead: amlodipine's vascular selectivity and
  verapamil's cardiac selectivity are both applied that way, and both say so.

- **Sixteen drugs have no receptor targets, every one for a stated reason.** GtoPdb
  publishes no human affinity for amiodarone at hERG, for metformin at AMPK, for
  omeprazole at the proton pump, or for methamphetamine and MDMA at anything at all.
  Those drugs behave correctly through cited direct effects, and the receptor panel
  honestly shows nothing bound. `MISSING_CONSTANTS.md` §2 lists each with its reason.

- **No metabolites, still.** It matters more across a wider set: morphine-6-glucuronide,
  oxypurinol, and the active metabolites of spironolactone, fluoxetine and diazepam all
  outlast their parents, sometimes by days.

- **The controlled substances model pharmacology, not context.** Tolerance, dependence
  as a developing state, withdrawal, and route-dependent abuse liability are all absent.
  `neuro.dependence` accumulates as an effect-bus quantity and nothing reads it yet.

## 7d. Food and nutrition

The food set grew from 8 hand-written entries to 46 USDA-sourced ones, each carrying its
FoodData Central identifier. Three fields were added that the gastrointestinal model now
uses, and one that it does not.

**Glycaemic index scales the RATE of glucose absorption and nothing else.** The parcel
still gives up all of its carbohydrate eventually, so the index changes the shape of the
curve and not the area under it. That distinction is the whole point: an index that
altered total absorption would be a fudge factor on energy rather than a model of how
fast a food is digested, and `tests/sim/nutrition.test.ts` asserts the area is preserved.

**Fibre slows absorption in proportion to the parcel's own carbohydrate**, because 5 g of
fibre alongside 10 g of carbohydrate is a quite different food from 5 g alongside 80 g.
It is conserved across gastric emptying and travels with the carbohydrate it accompanies.

**Alcohol and caffeine are handed to the drug pharmacokinetics**, not modelled separately.
A drink is the route by which a body receives ethanol, so it goes through the same oral
path any tablet uses and arrives with the zero-order elimination its own drug entry
declares. Two glasses of wine therefore interact with a benzodiazepine, because both
reach `neuro.sedation` by the same effect bus.

One consequence worth stating, because it is the check that the shared path is real:
a single standard drink produces a peak of about 208 mg/L, which is 0.021% blood
alcohol — the figure a 70 kg adult actually reaches — and a cup of coffee produces
about 2 mg/L of caffeine at an hour, which is the published peak for 95 mg. Neither
number was fitted. They fall out of the volume of distribution and the elimination each
substance declares in the drug data.

### What is still missing

- **Osmolality is not modelled**, although it is a genuine independent driver of gastric
  emptying: a concentrated drink empties more slowly than a dilute one of the same
  volume and sugar content. The data carries sodium and potassium for several foods, so
  the input exists; the model does not use it.

- **The glycaemic-index scale factor is an engineering choice.** Indices are measured and
  cited; the mapping from an index to a rate multiplier — normalised against 55 and
  clamped to a factor of four either way — is not. It has the right sign and a plausible
  magnitude, and it is not a measurement.

- **Sodium, potassium, calcium and iron are carried in the data and mostly unused.** Only
  iron reaches the model, through the existing ferrous sulfate payload path. A salty meal
  does not raise serum sodium, which it should.

- **No protein-specific handling beyond gastric buffering.** Amino acids are absorbed as
  an aggregate and do not feed gluconeogenesis, insulin secretion or the thermic effect
  of food.

- **No vitamins, no micronutrients, no absorption interactions.** Calcium does not impair
  iron absorption; nothing is modelled that would let it.

- **Portion scaling is linear and capped at six.** Real gastric accommodation is not
  linear, and the model has no upper limit on what a stomach will hold — a limitation
  already recorded under the gastrointestinal section.

## 7e. Hormones

See `docs/ENDOCRINE.md` for the full treatment. The headline gaps:

- **There is no pituitary**, so the TSH-thyroxine feedback loop is not closed and
  thyroid disease is not a reachable state.
- **Six hormones**, not the twenty-odd a complete model would carry.
- **Adrenaline is not endocrine here** — sympathetic drive acts through the baroreflex,
  so exogenous catecholamines do not compete with an endogenous adrenal output.
- **Calcium is unregulated**: no PTH, no calcitonin, so a calcium load does not return.

## 7f. The vascular overlay

See `docs/VASCULAR.md`. It is a **drawing**, not a model — nothing in it affects a
computed number. The important caveats:

- **The vessel tree is not connected.** Particles wrap within a segment rather than
  flowing on into the next. What you see is each vessel flowing, not a circuit traced.
- **Flow is identical in every segment**, because the model has one systemic resistance.
  A renal artery carries particles at the aorta's speed, which is wrong.
- **Radii never change.** Vasoconstriction is a number in the cardiovascular model and
  is not drawn.
- **No capillaries.** Thirty-one named macroscopic segments; all the exchange that
  matters physiologically happens below that resolution.

## 7g. Infection, environment and the wider body (2026-09-24)

- **A pathogen is a logistic burden, not an organism.** Ten cited pathogens grow toward
  a carrying capacity and are checked by one innate and one adaptive term. There is no
  site of infection beyond what each pathogen writes onto the bus (shunt, leak, fever,
  diarrhoea, haemolysis), no source control, no abscess and no resistance emerging under
  treatment.
- **Antimicrobial kill is an Emax on free concentration against one MIC per pathogen.**
  Wild-type MICs (EUCAST) are used; acquired resistance is not modelled, so an
  in-spectrum drug always works. Antivirals use a cell-culture EC50 in place of an MIC,
  which the source notes say can vary several-fold with the assay.
- **Some things are too slow to watch.** HIV suppression and thyroxine's metabolic
  effect take weeks in reality; they run on their real clocks here and are stated rather
  than sped up.
- **The environment is acute.** Altitude, FiO₂ and ambient temperature act at once;
  there is no acclimatisation beyond the kidney's slow bicarbonate compensation (no
  2,3-DPG shift, no rise in haematocrit over days, no heat acclimatisation).
- **Anaphylaxis is two mediator pools.** Histamine (which an antihistamine competes for)
  and a lumped non-histamine pool (which it cannot touch); there is no biphasic
  reaction and no tryptase readout.
- **Unit-dosed biologics are carried in milligrams** by their labels' conversions
  (insulin 1 IU = 0.0347 mg, heparin 180 USP units/mg, vasopressin 530 units/mg), because
  the engine's pharmacokinetics are mass-based. The unit label is kept on each preset.

## 8. Pharmacodynamics

- **Receptor effect gains are calibrated, not measured.** Their sign and relative
  magnitude come from the cited pharmacology; the absolute scale was fitted so that a
  reference dose produces a textbook-sized response. Every gain carries its citation
  and this caveat.
- **`ec50Occupancy` encodes receptor reserve** rather than being measured. See
  `DECISIONS.md` ADR-007 for why a value of 0.5 would be wrong.
- **Blood–brain barrier access is a coarse physicochemical rule, overridden where it
  is known to be wrong.** The rule cannot see efflux pumps, permanent charge or small
  polar molecules that cross freely, so it is overridden from labels or measurements
  for ipratropium, glycopyrrolate, loratadine, cetirizine (PET brain H1 occupancy),
  caffeine and theophylline (both label "CSF approximates plasma"). A drug with no
  value gets full central access. Since 2026-09-25 penetration gates each receptor's
  CENTRAL effects only (per-effect flag, `tools/ingest/central_effects.ts`) and a drug
  reaches peripheral effects fully; before that one blended factor let barrier-excluded
  drugs keep a quarter of the central action.
- **Central antimuscarinic action is too strong.** Full central muscarinic blockade
  reads as roughly a 40 % loss of consciousness, so atropine 1 mg (which crosses the
  barrier) scores about 0.57. Volunteers given 0.5-4 mg show dose-dependent psychomotor
  slowing, not obtundation (Ellinwood 1990); no quantitative source was found to
  recalibrate the M1/M4/M5 arousal gains, so they stand, and this is the caveat.
- **Inverse agonism is not distinguished from neutral antagonism.** A bound receptor
  signals between nothing and full, so an inverse agonist silences the receptors it
  holds exactly as a neutral antagonist does. There is no separate constitutive-activity
  term for it to suppress below that. (Treating IA = -1 as a negative full-agonist unit,
  as the engine did until 2026-09-25, let 12 % brain H1 occupancy by cetirizine remove
  80 % of histaminergic tone.)
- **Resting histaminergic vascular tone is overstated.** H1 carries one resting tone for
  all its effects, so blocking it raises systemic resistance by ~20 % in a healthy body
  and the baroreflex slows the heart a few beats - antihistamines do nothing measurable
  to a resting pressure in reality.
- **One receptor per family.** α1A/B/D are one receptor; so are α2A/B/C, the
  muscarinic subtypes beyond M2 and M3, and the NMDA subunit combinations. No drug
  in the shipped set discriminates them meaningfully, but one could.
- **Receptors have no desensitisation, internalisation, or up/down-regulation.** A
  continuous infusion produces a sustained effect where tachyphylaxis would occur.
- **Transporter semantics are simplified.** A reuptake inhibitor and a substrate
  releaser both raise synaptic transmitter; the releaser is modelled as twice as
  effective per unit occupancy. The real mechanisms are not equivalent.
- **The Pulse pharmacodynamic block is translated, not adopted.** Pulse modifies
  systolic and diastolic pressure directly; CORPUS computes them from a circuit, so
  the mean modifier is routed to systemic resistance and the pulse-pressure component
  to contractility. That translation is an interpretation.
- **Endogenous ligands are a scalar tone, not molecules.** There is no circulating
  noradrenaline concentration; β1 tone is a number driven by the reflex limb.
- **There is no effect-site delay.** Binding equilibrates with plasma, so a drug whose
  effect lags its plasma level (digoxin over hours, atropine's central action, thyroxine
  over days) acts on the plasma clock.
- **Hormone drugs act once.** A drug that is a hormone and binds that hormone's receptor
  (vasopressin, hydrocortisone, glucagon) acts through the receptor and only adds to the
  hormone's MEASURED level; levothyroxine, which has no receptor here, acts through the
  hormone pool. Until 2026-09-25 the first three acted through both and were counted
  twice.

## 9. Metabolic and endocrine

- **Bergman's minimal model**, with one substitution: the canonical secretion term
  `γ(G−h)⁺·t` is only defined for a single intravenous glucose tolerance test and is
  meaningless for a body that eats repeatedly. A first-order secretion rate is used
  instead, calibrated so a post-prandial glucose of 140 mg/dL gives about 60 µU/mL.
- **Six hormones as first-order pools** (cortisol with a circadian rhythm, aldosterone,
  ADH, thyroxine, glucagon, erythropoietin). There is no growth hormone, no pituitary
  (TSH is a constant, so thyroxine sits at its set point) and NO NEGATIVE FEEDBACK by
  hormone level: a hydrocortisone course does not suppress the adrenal's own output.
- **The glucose model's insulin feedback is stiff.** The explicit, insulin-suppressed
  hepatic output sits on top of Bergman's minimal model, whose remote insulin action
  already carries insulin's suppression of production, so insulin's brake on the liver
  is partly counted twice. Glucagon's glycogenolysis is therefore an additive term that
  insulin does not suppress, and its receptor gain (25) was calibrated so 1 mg SC
  reproduces the label's mean peak of 136 mg/dL; the gain describes this model, not the
  receptor. The two unsourced coefficients of the original code (insulin suppression
  slope 0.055 per µU/mL, counter-regulation gain 2.2) stand; clamp data put half-maximal
  suppression of glucose production at 29 µU/mL (Rizza 1981), about twice the model's.
  Replacing the model with one that separates production from uptake is the real fix.
- **Lactate is produced from an oxygen-delivery deficit** against a fixed critical
  threshold. There is no aerobic glycolysis, no exercise lactate, and clearance is a
  single first-order term standing in for hepatic and renal Cori-cycle activity.
- **Temperature is one well-mixed body.** No core-to-shell gradient. Shivering and
  sweating (capped at 1 L/h, Sawka 2007) respond proportionally from the set point with
  no interthreshold band, so the smallest heat load sweats a little. There is no
  circadian temperature rhythm.

## 10. Neurological

- **The EEG is a descriptive band model**, not a neural-mass model. Five oscillators
  whose relative power is set by consciousness and sedative occupancy. Burst
  suppression is a scripted envelope above 80 % sedation.
- **Consciousness is one number.** There is no Glasgow Coma Scale. It is full above
  the syncope threshold of cerebral flow and graded to nothing at no flow (the same gate
  the brainstem's drive uses), so between about 0.3 and 0.55 of resting flow - where the
  real cortex has already failed - it reads a little too awake. Pupils and seizures
  exist (`systems/mind.ts`); the psychedelic mydriasis that 5-HT2A mediates does not,
  because no source gave its size.
- **Posture changes brain perfusion by a fixed column.** Upright, cerebral perfusion
  pressure is heart-level MAP minus 22 mmHg of hydrostatic column (30 cm, Rao 2013) plus
  the measured fall of intracranial pressure to -2.4 mmHg (Petersen 2016). Stature does
  not change the column, and there is no falling down: a body that greys out standing
  stays standing.
- **Cerebral autoregulation is a static curve with CO₂ reactivity.** No intracranial
  pressure, no cerebral oedema, no Cushing reflex.

---

## 11. Fluids

- **The body drinks to thirst rather than on a schedule.** Obligate intake is a
  constant that balances obligate output at baseline. It does not respond to
  osmolality, hypovolaemia or the thirst that a real dehydrated body would feel.
- **A water load barely raises urine output.** ADH now falls when plasma is dilute, but
  the kidney scales water reabsorption with a very small gain, so even no ADH at all
  raises urine flow by about a fifth where a real water diuresis reaches ten times
  basal. Modelling urine as osmolar excretion over an ADH-set urine osmolality (50-1200
  mOsm/kg) is the fix.
- **The interstitium will not drain below half its baseline volume.** Past that point
  a real body is in profound shock and this model has nothing useful left to say.
- **Electrolytes redistribute by a damped dilution factor**, not by osmotic
  equilibration across cell membranes.

---

## 12. Emergency and procedures

- **Defibrillation success is probabilistic**, conditioned on rhythm, downtime,
  coronary perfusion pressure, energy and fibrillation amplitude. The base rate is
  taken from first-shock termination statistics for biphasic devices; the
  conditioning factors are calibrated.
- **Shocking asystole and PEA does nothing**, correctly and deliberately.
- **There are no reversible causes to treat.** No tension pneumothorax, no tamponade,
  no toxins, no hypothermic arrest. The "H's and T's" are absent.
- **CPR quality is rate only.** Depth, recoil and interruption fraction are not
  modelled, and the compression pressure amplitude is fixed.
- **Post-arrest physiology is missing.** There is no reperfusion injury, no
  post-arrest myocardial stunning and no neurological outcome.

---

## 13. Rendering

- **Placeholder geometry.** Organs are procedural approximations, correct in
  position, scale, pivot and bounding sphere and wrong in every detail. This is by
  design: the material has no diffuse or specular term, so surface detail is
  invisible and a smoothed placeholder renders identically to a scan. Phase 7
  replaces the meshes without touching anything else.
- **The exploded vertical stack is not anatomy.** The detached floating brain is a
  deliberate design choice, kept in `organs.json` so it stays data-driven.
- **Only the selected organ and its interior contents write depth.** Dormant organs
  never occlude each other, which is what makes the absorption compositing exactly
  order-independent. It also means a dormant organ behind another is fully visible
  through it, which is not how an opaque body works.
- **The fluid level is a world-axis-aligned horizontal plane.** Tilting the body
  would not tilt the fluid surface, because the body cannot be tilted.

## 14. Transport and performance

- **Without cross-origin isolation the waveform channel degrades.** The 250 Hz ring
  needs a `SharedArrayBuffer`, which needs COOP and COEP headers. Without them the
  client polls a transferred `Float32Array` every 50 ms: the traces still work, but
  they arrive in bursts rather than continuously. The footer says so when it happens.
- **Wall-clock catch-up is capped at 250 ms.** A backgrounded tab drops its backlog
  rather than running six thousand ticks in one frame. Simulated time is lost, not
  compressed.
- **At 300× the worker may not keep up on slow hardware.** There is no feedback that
  says so beyond the tick-cost readout in the body panel.

## 15. What would be misleading to conclude from this simulation

- That a particular dose is safe or appropriate for any real person.
- That a drug's effect size here predicts its effect size in a patient.
- That the absence of a modelled adverse effect means the drug does not have it.
  Most adverse effects are not modelled at all.
- That a successful resuscitation here says anything about a real one.
