## 5. Cross-check drift (curated value vs. the openFDA SPL parse)

The SPL parser reads section 12.3 prose. It is advisory: a disagreement here means
the parse picked a different number (often a paediatric or hepatic-impairment figure),
or that the curated value should be revisited. It never overwrites a cited value.

**This list is the one that needs work.** A disagreement nobody has ruled on is
indistinguishable from one nobody has read. Once it has been ruled on it moves to
5.1 with its reasoning attached, so this table is expected to be empty.

_No unadjudicated disagreements._

### 5.1 Adjudicated disagreements

Each of these has been examined and ruled on in `tools/ingest/drift_adjudication.ts`.
Nothing here is suppressed: the comparison still runs, the disagreement is still
printed, and if a curated value changes or the parser starts matching a different
sentence the ruling stops applying and the row returns to 5 as unexplained.

The pattern is worth noting. In five of the six the curated figure and the parsed
figure come from **the same document**: a human read the label and took the sentence
about the drug, while the regex took one about a metabolite, an enantiomer, a
different drug class, or a different experiment. That is a limit of reading prose
with a pattern, not evidence that the curated value is wrong.

**`esmolol`** — curated stands

> terminal half-life (min) vs the SPL prose parse: curated 9.00 vs SPL-parsed 222 (ratio 24.67). Label sentence: "The acid metabolite has an elimination half-life of about 3.7 hours and is excreted in the urine with a clearance approximately equivalent to the glomerular fil"

The sentence the parser matched is about the ACID METABOLITE, not about esmolol: "The acid metabolite has an elimination half-life of about 3.7 hours." Esmolol itself is hydrolysed by red-cell esterases with a half-life near 9 minutes, and that ultra-short duration is the entire clinical point of the drug — it is why it is given as an infusion and why it is chosen when a beta blocker may need to be stopped in a hurry. A 3.7 hour esmolol would misrepresent the one property it is selected for.

**`ketorolac`** — curated stands

> terminal half-life (min) vs the SPL prose parse: curated 330 vs SPL-parsed 150 (ratio 0.45). Label sentence: "The half-life of the ketorolac tromethamine S-enantiomer was approximately 2.5 hours (SD ± 0.4) compared with 5 hours (SD ± 1.7) for the R-enantiomer."

The label reports the two enantiomers separately — "approximately 2.5 hours for the S-enantiomer compared with 5 hours for the R-enantiomer" — and the parser took the first number in the sentence. Ketorolac is administered as a racemate, and the slower R-enantiomer dominates the terminal phase, so the curated 5.5 hours describes the drug that was actually given. Neither number is wrong; they are half-lives of different molecules.

**`hydromorphone`** — curated stands

> protein binding: curated 0.200 vs SPL-parsed 0.135 (ratio 0.68)

Both figures sit inside the published 8-20% range and the disagreement is an artefact of the tolerance being proportionally tight at small values: 0.135 against 0.200 trips a 0.4 ratio test that 0.55 against 0.80 would not. What the model consumes is the FREE fraction, 0.865 versus 0.800 — under 9% apart, which is far inside the spread of the affinity data it feeds. Kept as the curated value for consistency with the other opioids in the set rather than because it is measurably better.

**`metformin`** — curated stands

> protein binding: curated 0.0200 vs SPL-parsed 0.380 (ratio 19.00)

A parse error on a sentence whose subject is a different drug class. The label says metformin is negligibly bound to plasma proteins and draws a contrast with the sulfonylureas, which are more than 90% bound; the regex matched inside the contrast clause. Metformin being essentially unbound is not a marginal reading of the label, it is the point the sentence is making.

**`cannabidiol`** — curated stands

> terminal half-life (min) vs the SPL prose parse: curated 1080 vs SPL-parsed 3510 (ratio 3.25). Label sentence: "Elimination The half-life of cannabidiol in plasma was 56 to 61 hours after twice-daily dosing for 7 days in healthy subjects."

The two numbers describe two different experiments and both are real. The label figure is "56 to 61 hours after twice-daily dosing for 7 days" — a steady-state terminal estimate dominated by slow redistribution out of adipose tissue once the deep compartment is loaded. CORPUS administers single doses, where the observed plasma phase is nearer the curated 18 hours. Adopting the repeat-dose figure would give a single dose of cannabidiol a two-and-a-half day tail that nobody would measure after one dose.

**`methamphetamine`** — curated stands

> terminal half-life (min) vs the SPL prose parse: curated 630 vs SPL-parsed 270 (ratio 0.43). Label sentence: "The biological half-life has been reported in the range of 4 to 5 hours."

The label sentence is about a different quantity: "The BIOLOGICAL half-life has been reported in the range of 4 to 5 hours" is a duration of effect, not a plasma elimination half-life, and the parser cannot tell the two phrases apart. The curated 10.5 hours comes from a peer-reviewed clinical pharmacology review (Cruickshank and Dyer, Addiction 2009, "half-life 10-12 h"), which is a stronger source for a plasma constant than a label sentence about how long the effect lasts. This is also the only one of the six where the curated value does not come from the same document as the parse.
